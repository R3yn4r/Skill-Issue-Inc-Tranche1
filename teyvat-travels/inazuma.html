The nation of eternity

An island realm shaped by thunder and memory

Inazuma is an island nation set apart by the force of its storms and the discipline of its traditions. The landscape shifts between sky-etched mountain ridges, shrine-filled forests, and coastal settlements where the tension between rule and rebellion still hangs in the air.

At the heart of the realm stands the Raiden Shogun, whose long rule has shaped life across Inazuma. Every path through the islands carries a sense of history — of old gods, military conflict, and a pursuit of eternity that continues to divide and define the people.

Points of interest

Key Locations

Narukami Island Grand Shrine

The largest and most important shrine in Inazuma, located at the very summit of Mt. Yougou. Overseen by Guuji Yae Miko, it houses the Sacred Sakura—a massive, mystical tree whose roots spread throughout the entire nation to protect it from dark corruption.

Best for: Sacred Sakura

Ritou Trading Port

The heavily regulated trading port that serves as the only entry point for foreigners arriving in Inazuma. Managed by the Kanjou Commission, it was famously the choke point during the Sakoku Decree, where outsiders were confined to the island and heavily taxed just to do business.

Best for: History

Tenshukaku

The towering architectural centerpiece of Inazuma City and the official residence of the Raiden Shogun. Perched at the highest point in the capital, it overlooks the entire island as an absolute symbol of the Electro Archon's authority and her pursuit of Eternity.

Best for: Architecture

Grand Narukami Shrine

The largest shrine in Inazuma, located at the peak of Mt. Yougou. It is dedicated to Her Excellency, the Almighty Narukami Ogosho. For those who have wishes, they can climb to the top of the mountain one step at a time. For those who have gained the favor of Electro, they can use the Thunder Spheres to fly up and reach the shrine surrounded by the huge Sacred Sakura Tree. The Sacred Sakura is in constant bloom, symbolizing the eternity of Narukami. After passing through the many torii gates, find the shrine maiden to draw a fortune slip and ask for Narukami's blessing.

Best for: Blessings

Tatarasuna, Kannazuka

Legend has it that Kannazuka means "the hill where no gods reside." Until now, Kannazuka is disputed territory between the Shogunate and Sangonomiya. Kannazuka has a mountainous terrain with high steep cliffs. In the midst of the rocky outcrops lies Mikage Furnace, the largest smelting facility in Inazuma. This is where Jade Steel is made, a rare material that is used in Inazuma to forge swords.

Best for: Industry

Kujou Encampment

The Shogunate's military camp in Kannazuka. There will always be an irreconcilable conflict between the people of Narukami who believe in the Raiden Shogun and the people of Sangonomiya who believe in Watatsumi Omikami. The strife between both parties has only worsened since the Vision Hunt Decree was enforced. To put down the Sangonomiya rebellion, the Tenryou Commission has stationed the Shogun's Army here. Compared to the Sangonomiya forces, the Shogun's Army has more troops, is better trained, and is well-equipped. Victory seems assured for the latter.

Best for: Military history

Musoujin Gorge

This narrow and majestic rift valley runs through the eastern part of Yashiori Island. This extraordinary sight is no natural formation, but rather is the result of the fight in which the Electro Archon slayed the giant serpent. The rift valley is named after the Musou no Hitotachi, a testament to the Electro Archon's skilled swordsmanship. To this day, the remains of the giant serpent are still scattered throughout Yashiori Island. The Tatarigami that emerged from this god's remains seem to be awakening...

Best for: Dramatic scenery

Serpent's Head

Of the giant serpent that was defeated by the Electro Archon with the Musou no Hitotachi, only its weather-bleached remains are left today. Although its skull still looks up at the sky defiantly, the god is already dead, and so the name of "Watatsumi Omikami" is gradually being forgotten. Even if the giant serpent is long dead, you can still clearly feel the traces of its power left behind. Under the influence of the Tatarigami, Yashiori Island has been deserted by its inhabitants. What can be seen on the island are just endless rain and thunderstorms trying to wash away the remnants of the past.

Best for: Legends

Nazuchi Beach

Located between Yashiori Island and Kannazuka, Nazuchi Beach is a place ravaged by war. On the shallow coast, apart from Violet Ibis looking for a place to rest, there are torn flags and broken arrows of prior battles, as well as broken masts and shattered decks of shipwrecks. As a result of the war, pirates and ronin infest the place searching for loot. Even the rare Dendrobium blooms in this place...

Best for: Battlefield history

Grand Narukami Shrine

The Grand Narukami Shrine stands as the spiritual center of Inazuma, located high above the island at the peak of Mt. Yougou. Pilgrims climb toward it with prayer and patience, while those favored by Electro may use Thunder Spheres to rise above the steps and reach the sacred grounds more quickly. The towering Sacred Sakura is in constant bloom, a symbol of Narukami's enduring eternity, and each torii gate marks the approach to a place where blessings are still sought.

Best for: Blessings

Inazuma

05

Tatarasuna, Kannazuka

Kannazuka is a contested, rugged landscape where steep cliffs and military presence dominate the view. It is here that the Mikage Furnace produces Jade Steel, one of the rare materials essential to inazuman craftsmanship, especially in sword-making. The island's history is marked by conflict and disputed spiritual loyalties, making it a place that feels both industrial and intensely political. Even the terrain seems built for struggle, with every ridge holding a story of power and resistance.

Best for: Industry

Inazuma

06

Kujou Encampment

The Kujou Encampment is the Shogunate's military base in Kannazuka, built to contain conflict and impose order in a region where systems of faith and loyalty are deeply divided. It reflects the broader crisis of Inazuma: a realm where the Raiden Shogun's authority and the Sangonomiya's beliefs are at odds. The camp's well-trained army and equipment make it clear that the conflict is not symbolic or distant, but active and ongoing, with victory seeming to lean heavily toward the Shogunate's side.

Best for: Military history

Inazuma

07

Musoujin Gorge

Musoujin Gorge is a dramatic rift valley cut through Yashiori Island, impossible to mistake for a natural accident. It marks the place where the Electro Archon defeated a giant serpent, and the scar left behind still feels active, as if the battle were not entirely over. The valley's name recalls the Musou no Hitotachi, and the surrounding landscape is layered with myth, broken remnants, and a growing sense that the consequences of that fight are still beginning to move.

Best for: Dramatic scenery

Inazuma

08

Serpent's Head

The remains of the serpent defeated by the Electro Archon are all that survive at Serpent's Head, weathered and bleached by storm and time. The skull still seems to face the sky in defiance, but the creature's power has already faded into memory. That emptiness is part of the place: rain, thunder, and Tatarigami linger over Yashiori Island as if the land still bears the weight of a battle long ago, even while the gods and names attached to it are slowly being forgotten.

Best for: Legends

Inazuma

09

Nazuchi Beach

Nazuchi Beach sits between the islands like a wound left behind by conflict. Broken arrows, torn flags, and shattered shipwrecks litter the shoreline, while pirates and ronin move through the area searching for salvage. In the midst of all that damage, even rare Dendrobium can bloom, as if the land itself refuses to become completely barren. It is a vivid reminder that Inazuma's beauty and its violence are never far apart.

Best for: Battlefield history

Before you go

Travel Tips

Where to stay

Inazuma City is the best base for a first visit, with easy access to markets, ferries, and major sanctuaries. It keeps your route flexible while staying close to the region's central power.

What to eat & drink

Look for grilled seafood, rice bowls, and shrine-side sweets made with local ingredients. Inazuma is most memorable when you eat simply and often while moving between islands.

Getting around

Use ferries for island travel, then depend on walking and thunder-powered routes for mountain sites and remote shrines. Weather and travel permits can change the pace of your trip quickly.
